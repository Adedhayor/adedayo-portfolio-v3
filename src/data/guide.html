the guide.

if i were starting over as a designer today, these are some of the things i’d keep close. a resource base for learning how to look, think, make, and keep going.

i’ve been meaning to clean this up for a while.

not because the resources are bad. mostly because the list grew the way these things usually do: one bookmark here, one recommendation there, and suddenly you have a small archive with no clear way in.

so this is me going back to the drawing board.

not as a perfect curriculum. not as a “become a designer in three weeks” plan.

before you start

there is a lot out there now.

more courses. more tools. more opinions. more people promising that their framework, template, or ai workflow is the thing that will finally make design make sense.

some of those things are useful. but none of them can replace the slow part:

looking closely. asking better questions. trying things. getting stuck. trying again.

this list is here to support that part. not to help you collect resources forever.

resource base

books

books are still one of the best ways to sit with an idea for longer than a few seconds.

these are the ones i’ve read and would recommend again:

  • clueless to designera beginner’s guide
  • refactoring ui
  • the design of everyday things

courses

honestly, the google ux certificate is still one i’d recommend as an introduction.

not because it tells you everything. it doesn’t.

but it gives you a useful way to think about problems, and that is what stayed with me. problems will always exist. the work is learning how to understand them, frame them, and make something better on the other side.

this is also a good place to use ai carefully: research what exists, compare the options, and find the thing that fits the work.

but studying icons can lead to something better than picking from a list. you start noticing weight, rhythm, proportion, and personality. eventually, you might even make a custom icon for something that needs one.

typography

how do people find fonts now?

sometimes it feels like we either use whatever a tool recommends or ask ai to choose for us. both can be useful, but you should still learn how to look for yourself.

someone once said something like: you probably only need five fonts. here are a few i keep coming back to:

  • recoletathis page’s headings
  • work sans
  • bricolage grotesque
  • satoshi
  • inter
  • gilroy

also, places to look

  • google fonts
  • dafonts
  • typeface

fonts change the voice of a design before you’ve written a single word. spend time with them.

motion

define what the interactions would look and feel like. think on click and the likes. here comes design engineering, where you take it from just being an artifact to a working prototype.

as simple as a button is, it’s a prototype. people click it. it has different states and also performs different actions. hence: cta. call to action.

digress. i call on y’all to please get your pvc and vote whenever you can. at any point in time. cos you matter.

things to make

resources are only useful when they eventually turn into practice.

so here are a few prompts. nothing too serious. just enough to get you making.

dribbble rebounds

pick a shot you like on dribbble. recreate it.

then make a rebound: your own version of the idea, not a perfect copy. document the process on dsgnsbydhayor if you want to show the trajectory.

website recreations

pick any website.

ignore the interactions for now. focus on the landing page and one other page. then make it responsive for mobile.

some places to start

  • mobimobile app framer landing page
  • riflesso

app screens

pick any two screens, from a web app or a mobile app, and recreate them.

other shots are optional. the point is not to finish a perfect case study. the point is to practise seeing, building, and explaining what you made.
